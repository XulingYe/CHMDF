From AI Motion Generation to Physical Humanoid Deployment: A Construction-Oriented Sim-to-Real Workflow

Preparing for ICCBEI 2027

Xuling Ye, Wenyue Deng, Markus König

Computing in Engineering, Faculty of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Ruhr University Bochum, Germany

Abstract

Humanoid robots have recently attracted significant attention as a promising automation technology for construction due to their ability to operate within human-designed environments and perform human-like movements. This capability creates an opportunity to extend robotic automation beyond highly structured, task-specific applications toward construction and building operation activities that remain largely dependent on human workers. However, developing and deploying humanoid behaviors for real-world construction applications remains challenging because humanoid motion development must bridge simulation and real robot systems while accounting for the physical constraints and environmental conditions of construction environments. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a Construction Humanoid Motion Deployment Framework (CHMDF) that establishes an integrated simulation-to-reality (sim-to-real) workflow for developing construction-oriented humanoid motions. The framework links construction task and scene information with text-to-motion generation, motion adaptation, simulation-based validation, and physical robot execution. The proposed workflow enables AI-generated humanoid motions to be systematically adapted, evaluated, and transferred to a physical humanoid platform while reducing development risks through simulation-first validation. A proof-of-concept demonstration is presented to verify the feasibility of proposed framework and provide a reusable foundation for future construction-oriented humanoid applications. The proposed approach contributes a structured methodology for bridging AI-based motion generation and real humanoid deployment, supporting future research on inspection, facility management, human-robot collaboration, and other construction-related tasks.
